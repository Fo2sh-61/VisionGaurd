# Verdict: what is, and is not, causing wrong plate readings

**27 Aug 2026.** Measured on the owner's EALPR set, 2,086 photos, 2,142 labelled
plates. Raw numbers in [eval-2026-08-27-theirs-fp32-fp16-trt.md](./eval-2026-08-27-theirs-fp32-fp16-trt.md).
This is the first time the plate reader has been measured at all.

## The three suspects, settled

| Suspect | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Our port diverges from gate-anpr** | **Ruled out.** | `theirs` vs `fp32`: **2086/2086, 100.0%** identical readings. Not "close" — every single photo, the same answer. |
| **FP16 precision** | **Real, and already reverted.** Costs 6.7%. | `fp32` vs `fp16`: 1946/2086, 93.3%. 140 plates read differently. |
| **The TensorRT plan** | **Real and severe, already reverted.** Costs 26.6%. | `fp32` vs `trt`: 1532/2086, 73.4%. **554 plates read differently.** |

## What that means

**The software is not the bug.** Our port reproduces gate-anpr exactly, on
gate-anpr's own data, on every one of two thousand photos. There is no
divergence left to find in `infer/plates.py` — rung 2 of the ladder in
[research.md](../research.md) §3 is closed without a single line changed.

**The 24 Aug configuration change was right, and is now measured rather than
argued.** `engine.yaml` already carries `half: false` and `accelerate: false`
for both plate models, with a comment reasoning it out. The reasoning was
correct and the size of the effect was larger than anyone guessed: a TensorRT
plan changes **one reading in four** on a model that classifies single
characters. That is not a tuning parameter, it is a different reader.

**Acceleration cannot be given back.** Rung 4 of the ladder asked whether the
speed could be recovered once accuracy was pinned. The answer is no, and it is
no by a wide enough margin that no FP32-engine experiment is worth building:
`build_engines.py` needs no `--fp32` flag, and **T018 is dropped** rather than
deferred. The plate models stay on the `.pt` at FP32. The cost is about 6 ms a
frame on the detector, which the config comment already accounted for.

**Finding the plate is not the problem either.** 99.8% recall across every
configuration. Whatever is going wrong at the gate, the detector is seeing the
plates.

## So why is the owner still seeing wrong numbers?

Three possibilities remain, and this dataset cannot separate them:

1. **The character model's real capability on plates it was not trained on.**
   Every number above is measured on the models' own training set. That is
   pipeline fidelity, not generalisation, and it is the one thing this dataset
   structurally cannot tell us.
2. **Plate size at the real gate.** Under 60 px the reader refuses; under
   100 px it reads with less confidence. The new width readout on the live
   boxes makes this visible for the first time — before today no gate tile had
   ever drawn a box at all.
3. **Angle, lighting, motion blur** at a real barrier, none of which the
   dataset's posed photographs contain.

## What would settle it

- **The confirmed-truth file** (`--label`, task T014, needs Mohamed). That
  turns "the configs agree with each other" into "and they are right",
  and produces the 95% figure P6-FR-037 asks for.
- **Site captures.** `python engine/tools/record.py` at the real gate, or
  Mohamed holding real plates to the camera and watching the new reader card.
  This is the only evidence that speaks to points 1–3 above.

Until then the honest statement is: **the reader is as good as the project it
came from, on that project's own data, and its accuracy on the school's gate
is unmeasured.** No number on any screen may say otherwise.
